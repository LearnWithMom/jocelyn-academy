1
Mission brief

Become a market detective

Predict before reveal

A stock price is an ongoing negotiation about what a business may be worth. Today’s price can move because new information changes what buyers and sellers expect next.

SeparateBusiness results from stock-price reactions
TraceNews through expectations and profit
JudgeWhether a headline changes the long-term story

Milo’s rule: Never begin with “Will it go up?” Begin with “What changed, and what did investors already expect?”

2
Learn visually

Price is a tug-of-war

New informationEarnings, products, rates, lawsuits, demand
→
Expectations changeFuture sales, costs, profit, and risk
→
Orders changeMore urgency to buy or sell
→
Price movesUntil buyers and sellers agree again

Good news can still send a stock down

What investors expected$1.20 profit per share
What happened$1.10 profit per share

The company made money, but less than investors expected. The surprise can matter more to the immediate price than whether the result sounds “good” by itself.

Signs she understands

She separates company facts from market reaction, uses expectations in her explanation, and knows that price, business quality, and value are related but not identical.

Useful follow-up questions

“What did investors appear to expect?” “Which part is evidence?” “What would make you change your conclusion?”

Common misconceptions

More buyers than sellers is shorthand for greater buying urgency at current prices; every completed trade still has both a buyer and seller. Good companies can be overpriced. Falling prices do not prove a company is bad.
